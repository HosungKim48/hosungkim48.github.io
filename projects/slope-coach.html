How it works

Three steps, a few seconds

The phone is the measuring tool. Laid flat beside the ball with its top end pointing at the target, it measures the tilt toward the target and across the stance, so the reading matches where the golfer stands.

Step 1

Lay the phone down

Tap the shutter and set the phone beside the ball. A spirit level shows the tilt live, and after one second of stillness the app names the lie, for example "Uphill, medium: 6.5° toward the target."

level < 2°mild 2–5°medium 5–9°steep ≥ 9°
Step 2

See pro tips

A four-line tip for that exact lie: Ball, Stance, Aim and Flight. A mixed lie, such as "Downhill + ball above feet, steep," gets advice that combines both slopes. Swing clips for the lie sit right below.

Step 3

Tell us if it helped

After the shot, one tap on thumbs-up or thumbs-down. A thumbs-down asks why: wrong slope, unclear, or didn't help. Every rating is tied to the lie, so the tips keep getting better.

Ball

Where to play it in the stance.

"Play it back of centre, hands ahead of the ball."
Stance

How to set the body to the slope.

"Widen stance, tilt shoulders with slope, grip down."
Aim

Club choice and where to aim.

"One less club, aim a little right of the target."
Flight

What the ball will do.

"Expect it lower, with more roll, curving left."
Features

Built for the 15 seconds before a shot

Everything a golfer needs mid-round and nothing more: quick to read, works in sunlight, and doesn't need a signal for the slope read or the tip.

Spirit-level slope read

Uses the phone's motion sensor, so it works on every iPhone, with no camera or LiDAR needed. It locks after one second of stillness.

49 lie types

Uphill, downhill, ball above or below the feet, and every mix of them, at four severity levels. Each has its own tip.

Four-line tips

Ball, Stance, Aim, Flight. Each tip is checked by code against the slope math: club up or down, aim side, and which way the ball curves.

10-second calibration

Phones don't lie perfectly flat because of the camera bump or a case. Calibration removed a 1.08° tilt on the test phone.

Left- and right-handed

Left-handers stand on the other side of the ball, so the app flips "ball above / below feet" and mirrors the tips.

Swing clips per lie

Short swing videos for each lie sit below the tip. In the partner version these are coach-licensed clips.

Ratings that teach the system

Every thumbs-up or thumbs-down, with an optional reason, is tied to the lie type, so weak tips can be found and fixed.

Private by default

No account, no photos and no location in the log. Usage data stays on the phone until the golfer chooses to send it.

Book a coach

When a golfer keeps missing the same lie, the app shows a coach who teaches it, with a "Book a lesson" button.

Why it gets stronger with use

More scans → better data. Every rating is tied to one of 49 lie types, so over time the data shows which lies each golfer keeps missing.

Better data → better matches. A golfer who keeps missing ball-below-feet shots sees the coach whose ball-below-feet clips rate best.

Better matches → more partners. Coaches go where lessons get booked, and golf apps want the feature with the best content.

Reading tilt from a phone is easy to copy. The ratings history across lies, tips and coaches isn't.

FAQ

Questions partners ask

Short answers to what golf apps and coaches ask first.

Is it legal to use during a round?

For casual rounds and practice, yes. Under Rule 4.3 of the Rules of Golf, measuring slope with a device during a competition round is a penalty, and the score can't be posted for handicap. The feature is designed to switch off in tournament mode, the same way many GPS apps handle slope-adjusted distances.

How accurate is the reading?

After a 10-second calibration, which removes the phone's own tilt from the camera bump or a case, readings were right in all four directions in testing.

Who writes the tips?

Each of the 49 lies has its own tip, and code checks every tip against the slope math: club up or down, aim side, and which way the ball curves. Partner coaches can shape the wording for the lies they teach.

Whose swing clips are in the demo?

The demo shows public YouTube Shorts as examples, credited to their creators and played in YouTube's own player. In the partner version they would be replaced by clips licensed from partner coaches.

Does it need a server or an account?

No. The slope read, the lie name and the tip all run on the phone, and there is no account. The only network use is loading swing clips.
