Growing Anua’s PDRN line in the US

Anua, a Korean skincare brand, asked our student consulting team why its new PDRN serum and cream were selling far behind a key competitor on TikTok Shop, and what to change. We answered with content benchmarking, a US consumer survey and a pricing study, and turned it into a final report for Anua.

01

Two leaks in the funnel

Anua’s PDRN line was losing at both ends: its TikTok videos drew fewer views (a visit problem), and when shoppers reached the product page, a premium price wasn’t backed by the proof they look for (a purchase problem). The benchmark was Medicube, the strongest PDRN competitor on TikTok Shop in our data.

03

The plan: fix the visit, then the purchase

We turned the research into a customer-journey roadmap: content that stops the scroll, a product page that earns the premium, a bundle and price that lower the barrier to a first purchase, and a review engine that compounds over time.

Content · three video scenarios (hook → process → result)

Instant fix

Before & after × relatable story

HookOpen on the skin problem in close-up, then cut straight to the improved result.
ProcessEmotional caption (“the acne scars that made me want to hide”), then “and I found this cream” and how to apply it.
ResultClose-up of the finish; an honest line like “this one’s staying on my shelf.”
Lifestyle

Before & after × routine vlog

Hook“GRWM” or “PM skincare routine” with quick cuts of a productive day.
ProcessUnfiltered skin first, then the full routine with Anua’s products and the right amount per use.
ResultSkin from several angles; “I’m definitely keeping this in my routine.”
Sensory

Before & after × product texture

HookMinimal, “clean girl” aesthetic with ASMR sounds of the product.
ProcessBare skin from several angles, then a detailed application with texture close-ups.
ResultThe glow right after applying; “it just melts right into my skin.”

Price: finding the sweet spot (price-sensitivity meter)

How shoppers rate a new-ingredient serum or cream at each price

$15–19$20–24$25–29$30–34>$35
Expensive0%10%21%24%44%
Acceptable0%31%37%27%5%
Reasonable35%39%20%1%0%
Overlap35%70%57%28%5%

$20–24 is where most shoppers feel no resistance (70% overlap); $25–29 still works when the value is clear (57%). Against Anua’s $40 serum and $38 cream list prices, that pointed to an everyday discount of about 33% and 29%, deeper peak-sale discounts (about 45% and 42%), a first-purchase discount and a trial-size routine kit.

Top promotions under uncertainty: first-time purchase discount 31%, trial size 30%, bundle discount 21%, free gift 18%

Reviews · a three-step roadmap

Try

Seed high-quality reviews early with a targeted sampling program, sending the full routine rather than a single product.

Benchmark: Kérastase’s sampling program reached an 88% review rate and 1,000+ reviews

Buy

Tier the rewards by review type: a small code or points for text reviews, larger codes, credits or minis for photo and video reviews.

Survey: price (34%), number of reviews (32%) and promotions (19%) were the top purchase factors, ahead of brand (15%)

Use

Automate review requests after purchase, with two or three reminders to customers who haven’t reviewed yet.

Benchmark: Jane Iredale’s automated requests grew reviews 112%
